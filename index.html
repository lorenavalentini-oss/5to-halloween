READING · HOW HALLOWEEN CHANGED

1 · Long ago: More than 2,000 years ago, Celtic communities celebrated Samhain at the end of the harvest season. They believed that the boundary between the living and the dead became thinner at this time of year. People lit fires and sometimes wore costumes. Over many centuries, different traditions influenced the celebration that later became Halloween.

2 · Halloween today: Halloween takes place on October 31. In countries such as the United States, many children wear costumes and go trick-or-treating. Families decorate their homes with pumpkins, skeletons and spiderwebs. Some teenagers watch horror films or attend costume parties. Not everybody celebrates Halloween, and traditions vary between communities.

3 · A business opportunity: Halloween is also a commercial event. Shops sell costumes, sweets and decorations, and businesses advertise special products. Some people spend a lot of money, while others make costumes at home or reuse old decorations. Critics say that the celebration encourages unnecessary consumption. Supporters say that it brings people together and helps local businesses.

4 · Around the world: In Mexico, many families celebrate Día de Muertos on November 1 and 2. They remember loved ones who have died and create colourful altars. Although Halloween and Día de Muertos appear close together on the calendar, they are different celebrations with distinct histories and meanings.

5 · Right now: Imagine a school on October 31. Some students are preparing decorations, others are choosing costumes, and a group is organising a Halloween game. They are enjoying the activities, but they are also learning about the history behind the celebration.

📒 Grammar Notebook · Quick review

TO / SO AS TO
We use a scanner to create a digital copy.
Purpose: to / so as to + base verb
COMPARATIVES
A tablet is lighter than a desktop computer.
small → smaller; useful → more useful; good → better
PAST SIMPLE
Last year we created a Halloween poster.
yesterday, last year, in 2025; did + verb
WAS / WERE
There were pumpkins. The room was dark.
was: I/he/she/it; were: you/we/they
PRESENT SIMPLE
Our teacher uses a projector every week.
Routines and facts: usually, always, every year
PRESENT CONTINUOUS
We are designing costumes right now.
Actions in progress: now, at the moment, look!
RELATIVE CLAUSES
A programmer is someone who writes code.
who = person; which/that = thing; where = place; whose = possession
COMPUTING WORDS
We share a link and scan a QR code.
keyboard, monitor, projector, website, browser, file, backup
